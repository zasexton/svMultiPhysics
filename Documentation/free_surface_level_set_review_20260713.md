## Executive verdict

The original critical algebraic and geometric defects have been corrected inside a deliberately narrow sharp-interface P1 support envelope. Wet-extension velocity is now a sparse algebraic unknown in the coupled system, and constant-surface-tension unfitted interfaces default to a variational `SurfaceStress` force built from one generated-interface normal and measure. The later audit also corrected a coupled JIT/discontinuity-capturing defect: batched assembly had failed to restore each saved cut cell's diameter and volume into `EntityMeasures`, so a nonzero discontinuity-capturing contribution could be suppressed. Compiled combined, compiled vector-only, and interpreter residuals now agree. These are substantial infrastructure corrections, but the implementation is **not generally or quantitatively qualified for moving side-wall wetting**.

The strongest side-wall conclusion is now based on completed runs rather than timeouts. A true vertical `x=0` wall matrix used outward normal `(-1,0,0)`, tangential motion in `y`, and strong normal-only impermeability. All four advancing/receding cases completed 20/20 fixed steps at `n=16,32`, with no rejected step and converged recorded solves. Rotation against otherwise identical bottom-wall cases changes predicted speed, measured speed, geometric speed, and angle only at roundoff-to-small-discretization scale. The wall normal, tangential frame, and orientation-dependent postprocessing are therefore implemented consistently. The constitutive result is nevertheless wrong quantitatively: only `n=32` receding passes the fixed direct Ren--E speed gate; `n=16` advancing has the wrong sign, and the other two failed cases have relative speed errors from `0.60010` to `15.8991`. The defect is orientation independent and cannot be attributed to side-wall rotation alone.

The corrected static initializer samples the unmodified analytic circular cap at Q1 vertices; the earlier tangent-plane overwrite introduced local mesh-dependent kinks and is superseded. All six 60/90/120-degree sessile cases accept 3/3 steps. Every `n=32` row passes its per-mesh gates, while the `n=16` 90- and 120-degree rows fail contact-angle error. Refinement still fails the 60-degree pressure rate (`-2.8226`) and the 90-degree capillary-number rate (`-0.4569`), so the full static matrix remains open. The earlier closed-drop pressure error converges, but spurious speed remains nonmonotone and curvature error worsens, reinforcing that correct virtual work and pressure-sign algebra do not prove balanced static equilibrium.

The post-`EntityMeasures` `c7b297...` physical-evidence epoch `n=16` capillary wave completes 100/100 steps and passes every fixed scoped gate: maximum relative liquid-volume drift is `9.25622e-7`, frequency error is `1.2%`, and profile-amplitude error is `1.50241%`. The matching impermeable-wall advection pair completes 512/512 fixed steps at `n=8,16`, has zero false-liquid wall nodes or sign flips, and retains RMS refinement rate `2.04184`. These results belong to the named `c7b297...` epoch, not the final `e18f74...` executable; post-`c7b297...` verification targeted pressure-diagnostic sampling/cache/telemetry code, and that diagnostic was inactive in these physical runs. These close the two scoped transport gates; neither exercises the Ren--E contact law, supplies wave refinement, or establishes a complete discrete energy law.

The only currently supported dynamic-contact configuration is fixed-Eulerian `UnfittedLevelSet + CutVolume + LinearCorner` on a supported first-order parent cell with a scalar, continuous P1 level set; a stationary axis-aligned wall whose mapped physical normal is checked against the configured normal; strong normal-only impermeability; positive literal surface tension, mobility/friction, and Navier slip length; and the Ren--E line relation coupled to wetted-wall Navier dissipation. Fitted contact without a codimension-two entity, high-order or discontinuous contact geometry, unfitted level-set transport combined with ALE, variable surface tension/Marangoni forcing, complete-wetting endpoints, and full no-slip moving lines fail closed.

Within that envelope, contact terms reach the owning `equations` operator, the contact rule has true dimension `d-2`, and the through-liquid condition is `n·n_wall=-cos(theta)`. The continuum capillary traction remains `(-p_external-gamma*kappa)n`; the default unfitted constant-`gamma` discretization now represents its surface-tension part through `gamma*(I-n_g tensor n_g):grad(v)` using the same generated normal `n_g` and measure as the interface rule rather than an independently projected curvature. Level-set bound control never clamps or mutates an accepted solution: a would-be P1 one-ring violation rejects the candidate, after which adaptive stepping may reduce `dt` and retry or fixed-step operation fails explicitly.

The coupled nonlinear infrastructure has two explicit contracts. Configurations whose residual-defining external state changes inside a solve retain synchronized, transactional line-search refresh across generated geometry, affine constraints, current/history/rate state, and auxiliary, bordered, and locally condensed solver data. Algebraic wet-extension configurations instead refresh cut rules, curvature data, and the extension map at solve/accepted-step boundaries and freeze that package throughout Newton and line search. The extended velocity `E` remains an unknown: the level-set residual supplies `R_phi,E`, and sparse algebraic rows supply `R_E,u` and `R_E,E`, so the physical-velocity-to-level-set chain is exact for the frozen map. This is monolithic in `(u,E,phi)` at fixed topology, not an exact shape-differentiated Newton method.

Important gaps remain. `SurfaceStress` removes the independently projected-curvature force from the default unfitted constant-`gamma` path and pairs its generated conormal once with equilibrium wall energy and line friction. It does not provide contact-topology, point-location, measure, projector-normal, or cut-volume shape derivatives; generated geometry is intentionally refreshed-frozen. The constrained LSQR pressure/surface-load diagnostic assembles and records every requested full sample. Whenever the nonlinear contract requests an accepted-state residual refresh, that refresh is retained as its own sample even when current coefficients and time match. Only the mixed-pair/LSQR work is cached, and reuse requires revision-tracked mesh/geometry/constraints/sparsity, a tangent proven state independent, and an exactly unchanged reassembled constrained load. Production probing demonstrates fewer pair assemblies than diagnostic records. This proves safe and effective diagnostic reuse, not physical pressure/force compatibility: the coefficient-space least-squares residual still has no calibrated physical norm or acceptance threshold. Energy histories remain diagnostic proxies rather than a theorem.

FS-14 also remains open. A new fixed-physical-cut refinement fixture uses 2, 3, and 4 cells per axis and measures mass-normalized generalized pressure/velocity coupling singular values, stabilized Schur control, aggregation partition of unity/L1 weights/reach, and equilibrated mixed rank. It deliberately exposes a coarse failure: the unstabilized `n=2` Galerkin coupling has rank 6 for pressure dimension 8, while `n=3,4` are full rank; the stabilized finite controls pass all three levels. This is useful finite regression evidence, not a uniform inf-sup bound, an `h`-independent conditioning result, or theorem transfer from a different CutFEM/AgFEM space and stabilization.

D18/D38 include wall-compatible extension/transport guards, corrected thin-extrusion front/back symmetry, and the two parts of an open top boundary: natural momentum traction and an explicit level-set `LevelSetOutflow` declaration. Both decks request three face-neighbor ghost layers. Named level-set convergence now uses that equation's own absolute tolerance, defaulting to `1e-10`, instead of inheriting an unrelated stricter monolithic fluid tolerance; duplicates of the same field merge their own strictest effective criteria. The qualification profile separately sets the outer FSILS relative/absolute tolerances to `1e-10/1e-12` while retaining `1e-4` inner Navier--Stokes block tolerances. In the `c7b297...` physical-evidence epoch, D18 accepts one minimum-size substep at `t=1.5625e-5` and then rejects the next; D38 accepts two reduced substeps through `t=3.75e-4` before its fixed timeout interrupts the next solve. D38 also exposes a serious unresolved extension-map defect: refreshed tangential regressions can be severely amplifying even though rollback and the frozen-map algebraic tangent behave as designed. Because these cases have `gamma=0`, no contact law, and full no-slip on their physical side/bottom walls, they remain bulk transport/false-wet tests rather than wetting validation.

## Expected mathematical contract

For the current D18/D38 convention,

- liquid domain: Ω_l = {x : φ(x) < 0};
- free surface: Γ = {x : φ(x) = 0};
- outward liquid normal: n = ∇φ/|∇φ|;
- curvature: κ = div n, positive for a convex liquid circle or sphere;
- material-interface transport on a fixed Eulerian mesh: ∂φ/∂t + u_ext·∇φ = 0.

With liquid stress σ_l = −pI + 2μ ε(u), negligible gas viscosity, and external gas pressure p_ext, the free-surface traction for this curvature convention is

σ_l n = −(p_ext + γκ)n.

At static equilibrium this gives the Young–Laplace jump

p_l − p_ext = γκ.

Let n_w be the wall normal pointing out of the liquid computational domain and let θ be measured through the liquid. The geometric equilibrium condition is

n·n_w = −cos θ.

If an inward solid-to-liquid normal is used instead, the right-hand side is +cos θ. Either convention is valid, but the input documentation, geometry, diagnostics, and weak form must use one convention consistently.

The contact set C = Γ ∩ ∂Ω_w has Hausdorff dimension d−2: it is a line in 3-D and a point set in 2-D. A contact-line residual therefore integrates with dℓ in 3-D and a zero-dimensional counting measure in 2-D. It must not be passed through a codimension-one surface measure.

A prescribed static angle alone does not define a moving-contact-line model. A moving sharp contact line additionally needs a regularization and a constitutive relation, for example Navier/GNBC wall slip with a contact-line friction or dynamic-angle law, a diffuse-interface wall relaxation, a precursor/disjoining-pressure model, or an explicitly pinned line.

For constant `gamma`, the unfitted `SurfaceStress` residual uses the discrete surface-area variation

`integral_Gammah p_external*n_g.v + gamma*(I - n_g tensor n_g):grad(v) dGammah`.

With `div_Gamma(I - n tensor n) = -kappa*n`, this is equivalent to the interior curvature-traction residual and also produces the capillary conormal on an open interface. Consequently, the separate `SurfaceStress` contact term contains only equilibrium wall energy `-gamma*cos(theta_e)` and line friction; adding the full dynamic-angle gap would double count the dynamic conormal. This identity is a fixed-interface virtual-work statement, not by itself a time-discrete total-energy estimate.

### FS-01 — The prescribed contact-angle residual is absent from the production coupled solve

**Evidence**

- In the reviewed baseline snapshot, [IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp) constructed the unfitted angle residual and explicitly installed it on the **level_set** operator.
- [LevelSetEquationTranslator.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Application/Translators/LevelSetEquationTranslator.cpp) sent coupled level-set transport to **equations** when Coupled was true.
- [test_LevelSetEquationTranslator.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Application/Tests/Unit/test_LevelSetEquationTranslator.cpp) explicitly verified that the coupled module had **equations** and did not have **level_set**.
- [ApplicationDriver.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Application/Core/ApplicationDriver.cpp) selected **equations** for the steady residual/Jacobian and for transient Newton.
- The reviewed [D18 solver.xml](../tests/cases/fluid/open_vessel_free_surface/unfitted_level_set/spheric_test05_wet_bed_d18/solver.xml) had Coupled=true and Operator_tag=equations.
- Contact-angle unit helpers in [test_MovingDomainPhysics.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Tests/Unit/test_MovingDomainPhysics.cpp) manually created and assembled **level_set**, bypassing the production routing.

**Consequence**

In the reviewed baseline, the configured contact angle had no algebraic influence on a coupled D18/D38 Newton step. Generated marker diagnostics could be populated and the residual evaluated in isolation while the actual solution remained completely passive with respect to that residual. This was the most direct reason the baseline side-wall angle setting could not be trusted.

The fitted branch has the same integration inconsistency: it installs the prescribed-angle term on **mesh_motion**, while the coupled mesh-kinematic term is correctly installed on **equations**. A separate driver that explicitly solves mesh_motion could use it, but the unified ApplicationDriver path shown above does not.

**Required correction**

Install the angle contribution on the operator selected by the owning level-set or mesh-motion equation, preferably by passing the operator tag through the boundary model rather than hard-coding a string. Add an application-level integration test that assembles **equations** and proves that changing the target angle changes both residual and Jacobian rows for φ.

### FS-02 — Contact-line quadrature has the wrong codimension and an inconsistent frame

#### FS-02a: codimension-one remapping is applied to a codimension-two set

[Vocabulary.h](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Forms/Vocabulary.h) defined dInterfaceBoundary by returning integrand.dI(marker). [GeneratedInterfaceBoundaryIntersectionDomain.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Interfaces/GeneratedInterfaceBoundaryIntersectionDomain.cpp) consequently exported intersection rules as CutQuadratureKind::Interface.

[StandardAssembler.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Assembly/StandardAssembler.cpp) sent all such rules through the cut-interface surface remapper, which applied

- |det J| times the norm of J inverse-transpose n_ref in 3-D, which is a surface Jacobian; or
- |J t_ref| in 2-D, which is an edge Jacobian.

For a 3-D contact line, the correct mapping is |J t_ref|. For a 2-D contact point, the zero-dimensional measure is invariant and receives no length scale. The rule already stores a tangent, but the assembler ignores it.

Under a uniform mesh scaling h:

- a 2-D point contribution should scale as h to the zero power, but the current path scales it as h;
- a 3-D line should scale as h, but the builder first stores a physical line length proportional to h and the assembler then multiplies by a surface factor proportional to h squared, giving approximately h cubed.

The contact-angle penalty therefore changes with mesh size for purely geometric reasons.

#### FS-02b: reference coordinates are paired with physical weights and directions

The scalar-field path in [GeneratedInterfaceBoundaryIntersectionDomain.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Interfaces/GeneratedInterfaceBoundaryIntersectionDomain.cpp) collected current physical face coordinates and passed them to [ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Quadrature/ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp). That routine computed physical segment length, physical interface and wall normals, and a physical tangent.

When the request frame is Reference, the domain builder stores a reference quadrature-point coordinate but retains those physical weights, normals, and tangents at lines 917–927. toRule nevertheless labels the complete rule as Reference. StandardAssembler then:

- multiplies the already physical line weight by another geometry Jacobian; and
- transforms the already physical normal as if it were a reference normal.

This is a double mapping and a frame violation, independent of the codimension error.

The fallback builder also compares generated interface-fragment points expressed in reference coordinates with boundary faces collected in physical coordinates. That comparison is only valid for an identity mapping.

#### FS-02c: rule metadata overstates exactness

toRule reported exact_for_constants=true and achieved order equal to the requested order. High-order parent elements were reduced to linear corner geometry in [ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Quadrature/ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp), and the application callback supplied one scalar value per vertex. Higher Gauss order integrates a straight reconstructed segment more accurately; it does not make the interface/contact geometry high order.

**Required correction**

Introduce a distinct codimension-two rule kind or an explicit geometric dimension on generated rules. Then enforce exactly one of these contracts:

1. **Reference contract**: reference point, reference tangent/normal data, and reference d−2 weight; the assembler performs the appropriate codimension-two map once.
2. **Physical contract**: physical point, normal, tangent, and d−2 weight; the assembler performs no geometric remap.

Reject mixed data. Validate the implementation with affine stretch/rotation tests and uniform h-scaling tests in both 2-D and 3-D. The integral of one over a 2-D contact point must remain one; the integral over a 3-D straight contact line must equal its physical length.

### FS-03 — The fitted angle residual is applied on the entire free surface

The fitted branch in [IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp) formed the angle gap but integrated it with ds(bc.boundary_marker), the complete fitted free-surface boundary. It did not use contact_line_marker or wall_boundary_marker as the integration set.

In 3-D the prescribed angle belongs on the one-dimensional boundary of the free surface. In 2-D it belongs at the free-surface endpoint. Applying a wall-normal angle relation over the full surface is geometrically wrong even if the operator routing is repaired.

The pinned fitted model does use a contact-line constraint marker, demonstrating that the marker information exists. The prescribed-angle weak form must use an actual fitted contact-line entity or a boundary-of-surface formulation.

### FS-04 — Contact-angle sign and input convention disagree

[IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.h](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.h) said the angle was measured through the fluid phase. For φ < 0, the baseline code used +∇φ/|∇φ| as the liquid outward normal. D18/D38 configured wall normals −x, +x, −z, +z, which are outward normals of the liquid container.

The baseline residual in [IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp) enforced

n·n_w − cos θ = 0.

For the stated conventions it should enforce

n·n_w + cos θ = 0.

The baseline form would have been correct only if wall_n pointed from solid into liquid. Baseline tests encoded the implementation rather than the stated physics: a zero-degree target was considered matched when interface and outward wall normals aligned. The reviewed D18/D38 checks used 90 degrees, which could not detect the sign error.

**Required correction**

Choose and name the convention explicitly, for example wall_outward_normal. Derive the sign once from that convention, and test 60, 90, and 120 degrees for both LevelSetNegative and LevelSetPositive on every wall orientation. Diagnostics should report the actual through-liquid angle, not merely arccos of an unsigned or convention-dependent dot product.

### FS-05 — Capillary traction has the opposite Young–Laplace sign

[LevelSetCurvatureProjection.h](../Code/Source/solver/FE/LevelSet/LevelSetCurvatureProjection.h) states that κ = div(∇φ/|∇φ|) and is positive for an outward-positive circle or sphere. The fitted current-geometry curvature in [FrameGeometry.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Geometry/FrameGeometry.cpp) uses the same positive-convex convention. The active-negative unfitted path retains that sign.

The baseline dynamic stress code in [IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp) built

traction = (−p_ext + γκ)n.

For an outward liquid normal and positive convex curvature, it must be

traction = (−p_ext − γκ)n.

The baseline form predicted p_liquid = p_ext − γκ for a static sphere, rather than the Young–Laplace result p_liquid = p_ext + γκ.

The existing tests do not create a liquid pressure jump. They initialize the entire state, including pressure, to zero and show that an external-pressure residual cancels a capillary residual of the same numeric magnitude. That confirms the implemented opposing signs, but it is not a Laplace-law solution.

At the reviewed baseline, this defect did not cause the D18/D38 symptom because those cases set γ=0. It was nevertheless a critical error elsewhere in the free-surface infrastructure and is corrected in the authoritative update.

**Required correction**

Correct the traction sign and replace the cancellation test with a static circle/sphere whose liquid pressure is p_ext + γκ. Test the actual residual at that state. Also add a mesh-convergence study of parasitic currents, pressure jump, and surface-energy balance.

### FS-06 — Degenerate contact intersections fail open

[ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Quadrature/ImplicitBoundaryIntersectionQuadrature.cpp) handled more than two face cut points by sorting them by edge index and pairing adjacent entries. The fragments were tagged Ambiguous, but the baseline active predicate did not reject Ambiguous or EdgeAlignedZero. The generated domain mapped these statuses to TangentIntersection, whose active predicate also did not reject it. The overall status was then overwritten to Active if any such fragment contained quadrature.

Consequences include:

- a bilinear quadrilateral saddle can connect the wrong branches without an asymptotic/topological decider;
- a zero level set aligned with a boundary edge can be counted more than once by adjacent cells or faces;
- diagnostics can say Active while the only available geometry was ambiguous;
- the application strict check only fails when fragments exist but quadrature is empty, so ambiguous nonempty quadrature passes.

This contradicted the fail-closed behavior described in [FE/Docs/LevelSet.md](../Code/Source/solver/FE/Docs/LevelSet.md).

**Required correction**

Keep ambiguity in the final status and skip or reject it unless a deterministic topology rule resolves it. Deduplicate aligned contact entities by global topology. Add saddle-face, vertex-touch, edge-aligned, and MPI ownership tests that integrate constants and verify exact multiplicity.

### FS-07 — The unfitted path is not a moving-wetting model

Baseline validation in [IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp) rejected nonzero contact-line mobility, wall-slip models, and slip length. The only implemented term was a static geometric penalty on φ. The qualification log itself correctly excluded dynamic angle laws, wall energy/Young line forces, slip-coupled contact lines, and two-phase wetting.

At the reviewed baseline, the D18/D38 case imposed zero velocity on all six walls while asking the liquid/free-surface/wall intersection to evolve. With a material sharp interface, no slip kinematically pins a true contact line. Classical analysis shows that forcing a sharp contact line to move over a no-slip wall creates unbounded stress and dissipation; a numerical method must select and resolve a regularization.

Further inconsistencies in these cases are:

- surface tension is zero, so no Young capillary energy or line force exists to select a 90-degree equilibrium angle;
- the angle penalty is therefore purely numerical and has no compatible energetic scale;
- there is no wall condition for φ that controls interface orientation during transport or reinitialization;
- active wall vertices inherit the strong zero fluid velocity during velocity extension.

A static-angle penalty can be useful for a stationary or deliberately pinned contact line after its geometry and operator are corrected. It cannot by itself represent advancing/receding wetting in dam-break flow.

**Required model decision**

Before implementation, choose one supported physical regime:

1. **Pinned contact line**: impose line position, allow the apparent angle to evolve, and do not call it moving wetting.
2. **Sharp moving line with slip/friction**: Navier or generalized Navier wall condition, a contact-line friction/dynamic-angle law, and the associated Young line contribution in the momentum weak form.
3. **Diffuse/relaxed wall model**: phase-field or level-set wall relaxation with consistent wall energy and mobility.
4. **Engineering slip/hysteresis model**: a documented slip length and advancing/receding angle window with mesh-independent calibration.

### FS-08 — Nearest-interface velocity extension is unsuitable near walls

[ApplicationDriver.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/Application/Core/ApplicationDriver.cpp) constructed interface samples and assigned every dry vertex the velocity of the nearest Euclidean interface sample. This was not a solution of a normal-extension equation such as ∇u_ext·n = 0. It was:

- discontinuous in derivative at Voronoi boundaries;
- nonlocal and able to choose the wrong nearby interface branch;
- nonconservative and not differentiable with respect to φ or u;
- unaware of walls and contact-angle geometry;
- incompatible with impermeability unless explicitly projected to satisfy u_ext·n_w = 0.

At a baseline active wall vertex, the source velocity was copied directly. With the reviewed D18/D38 strong no-slip data, that value was zero, which pinned the true wall contact point while nearby dry nodes could still receive unrelated interface velocities.

The baseline Navier–Stokes module also contained a separate dry-region Laplace velocity extension with an arbitrary cut-volume floor. Thus two different extension concepts affected the coupled problem without a single mathematical contract.

This is a high-probability contributor to the observed dry-side sign changes, but the exact causal fraction needs the instrumentation tests listed later.

**Required correction**

Use a wall-aware extension PDE or closest-point construction that is constant along interface normals, respects wall no-penetration/slip, handles disconnected interfaces, and has a controlled narrow band. Test exact constant/linear fields, two nearby interfaces, wall corners, and moving contact lines.

### FS-09 — Level-set transport is under-stabilized at the wall/interface cliff

[LevelSetTransport.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/LevelSet/LevelSetTransport.cpp) implemented continuous-Galerkin advective or divergence form. SUPG used

τ = scale h / sqrt(|u| squared + epsilon).

It omits the usual transient scale, directional metric length, and any diffusion/reaction contribution. No discontinuity-capturing, crosswind diffusion, limiter, or bound-preserving mechanism controls overshoots. A steep gate/interface cliff can therefore develop oscillatory positive-to-negative nodal changes even where the physical local velocity is small.

Additional concerns:

- the divergence form moves the zero contour differently when the discrete velocity is not exactly divergence free;
- the optional interface kinematic term is an h-weighted copy of the same transport residual on dI, not an independent physical condition;
- only inflow data creates a boundary residual; impermeable walls have no φ orientation/contact-angle transport condition;
- every diagnostic mode is explicitly classified as not locally conservative.

SUPG is not inherently incorrect, but this particular scalar τ and lack of wall-aware shock control are not enough evidence for robust wetting/dewetting fronts.

### FS-10 — Projection reinitialization does not repair the interface neighborhood

[LevelSetReinitialization.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/LevelSet/LevelSetReinitialization.cpp) built linear interface primitives. With the automatic setting it preserved every degree of freedom within roughly 1.5 primitive diameters and only replaced farther values by a nearest-primitive signed distance. Thus the near-interface region—the region used for normals, curvature, cuts, and contact angle—was deliberately left unchanged even if |∇φ| differed from one.

This operation is not a Hamilton–Jacobi redistancing solve or a fast-marching method. The translator explicitly reserves those methods. More importantly:

- Reinitialization_max_iterations is parsed and stored but is never used by the projection algorithm;
- Reinitialization_pseudo_time_step_scale is also parsed and unused;
- baseline D18's configured max_iterations=4 therefore had no effect;
- linear primitives can propagate a noisy or topologically wrong interface fragment into the far field.

The repository's [D18/D38 root-cause report](d18_d38_spheric_test05_validation_root_cause_20260610.md) records that cadence-five projection reinitialization did not cure false wetting and moved its onset earlier, consistent with the above behavior.

**Required correction**

Rename the current method to describe what it does, reject inert options, and do not advertise it as iterative redistancing. For wall wetting, implement an interface-preserving redistance procedure with a compatible wall boundary condition and quantify zero-contour displacement, |∇φ| error, contact-angle error, and volume change.

### FS-11 — Global volume correction moves the contact line nonphysically

At the reviewed baseline, [LevelSetVolume.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/LevelSet/LevelSetVolume.cpp) found a scalar c by bisection and added c to every φ coefficient. D18/D38 applied this every accepted step to a tolerance of 1e−10.

A constant shift preserves ∇φ at fixed points but moves the entire zero contour, including every wall contact point, without reference to local velocity, wall impermeability, contact-line friction, or angle. If a small false-wet region appears, restoring total volume shifts the genuine free surface to compensate.

The prior D18 diagnosis measured shifts of roughly 1e−6 to 9e−6 per step and identified correction as an amplifier rather than the trigger. That distinction is important: disabling correction may hide global depression, but it does not repair the local transport error.

Use global shift as a diagnostic or carefully bounded mass repair, not as evidence of conservative interface motion. Report both uncorrected and corrected volume/contact-line displacement.

### FS-12 — Unfitted kinematic penalty represents the wrong condition on a fixed mesh

The module permits penalty kinematics for an unfitted interface and applies (u − mesh_velocity)·n = 0 in the momentum equation. On a fixed Eulerian background mesh, mesh_velocity is zero, so this forces u·n = 0 and freezes normal fluid motion at the interface. The correct Eulerian kinematic relation is the level-set transport equation.

D18/D38 do not currently enable this option, so it is not their error. The option should nevertheless be rejected for UnfittedLevelSet unless the supplied velocity is explicitly the physical interface velocity rather than mesh motion.

### FS-13 — Coupling is Picard/quasi-Newton rather than monolithic Newton

At the reviewed baseline, generated cuts, curvature, and extended advection velocity were refreshed at synchronization points but remained frozen through line-search trials. Unfitted shape tangents were disabled by default behind an environment flag. The transport Jacobian did not include the derivative of the nearest-interface extension with respect to fluid velocity or φ; that map is non-smooth in any case.

That could be a valid outer fixed-point strategy if convergence were measured on the fully refreshed residual, but it should not have been described or tested as an exact monolithic Newton method. Near a contact-line topology change, the frozen geometry could make a line-search residual inconsistent with its Jacobian and accepted trial. The current transactional synchronized-trial behavior and its remaining tangent limitations are recorded in the FS-13 completion evidence above.

Qualification should record outer refresh counts, refreshed residual norms, contact topology changes, and convergence under time-step and mesh refinement.

### FS-14 — Cut-cell pressure stability remains open

Several parts are sound:

- active-negative and active-positive cut-volume selection are consistently routed;
- pressure ghost stabilization remains after retirement of the excessive velocity ghost penalty;
- small-cut aggregation removes arbitrarily small supports and improves algebraic conditioning.

However, aggregation can homogeneously pin isolated cut-island velocity and pressure degrees of freedom, effectively deleting subresolution liquid components. That is a topology-removal policy, not a neutral stabilization, and it must be documented and measured.

The prior [open-vessel pressure-support report](open_vessel_free_surface_test02_test10_root_cause_report_20260605.md) identifies unresolved PSPG pressure-gradient support at free-surface cut rows, with weak velocity coupling and pressure-dominated boundary rows. This audit does not prove a new single-line defect there, but the current tests do not establish a cut-position-independent inf-sup or condition-number bound for the chosen continuous pressure, PSPG, pressure ghost penalty, and aggregation combination.

This remains a high-priority numerical qualification gap, especially when a pressure traction boundary, dry-side constraints, and moving aggregation bands interact.

### FS-15 — Lower-severity correctness and robustness gaps

#### Parameter validation

The parser accepts contact angle, penalty, mobility, slip length, kinematic penalty, surface tension, and wall normal values, but validation does not consistently require:

- finite scalar values;
- 0 ≤ θ ≤ π;
- positive contact-angle and kinematic penalty;
- nonnegative surface tension and slip length;
- finite, nonzero wall-normal components.

A negative penalty is accepted and changes a stabilizing constraint into an anti-penalty. Kinematic validation checks nonzero rather than positive.

#### Curvature recovery conditioning

[LevelSetCurvatureProjection.cpp](../Code/Source/solver/FE/LevelSet/LevelSetCurvatureProjection.cpp) fitted local quadratics through normal equations. Coordinates were not nondimensionalized and the dense solve used an absolute pivot threshold. Normal equations square the condition number, so recovery depended on mesh units and anisotropy. Neighbor fallback/smoothing could spread bad curvature through the adjacency graph. Curvature was then frozen during a nonlinear iteration unless supplied as a true unknown.

The guard that rejects raw curvature with nonzero γ unless a projected/supplied path is selected is good. It does not replace a convergence and parasitic-current study.

#### Generated marker collisions

Automatic contact markers are hashes reduced modulo a finite range without a collision registry. Distinct wall/intersection keys can silently share a marker and be integrated together. The probability is low, but correctness should not depend on it. Register key-to-marker ownership and fail on collision.

#### Higher-order geometry

At the reviewed baseline, canonical high-order cells were reduced to corner-only linear geometry for contact intersection while rule metadata reported requested order as achieved. High-order unfitted accuracy requires a curved/isoparametric interface reconstruction or an explicitly linear-geometry contract. Current D18/D38 P1 tetrahedra avoid this particular loss; the current supported path now fails unsupported high-order contact geometry closed and reports corner-linear order honestly.

#### Pressure datum and hydrostatic initialization

At the reviewed baseline, D18/D38 imposed a permanent pressure value at one interior vertex even though the free-surface traction supplied an absolute external pressure and dry-region constraints could already remove the algebraic nullspace. The constrained value was the initial hydrostatic value, so it remained fixed while the physical pressure field evolved. The prior D18/D38 ablation report measured a growing local checkerboard of roughly 400–1000 Pa around that vertex, although removing the pin alone did not measurably change the interface profile. The current decks remove this pin as documented in the authoritative addendum.

The baseline hydrostatic initializer was also not level-set aware: it filled pressure from one global top reference rather than the local liquid-column height. In the shallow wet bed, the prior report found initialization around 1292–1469 Pa where the local physical column supported at most about 176 Pa. The first incompressible projection largely removed this inconsistency, so it was not the false-wetting trigger, but it created a needless startup impulse and helped motivate an overconstraining pressure pin. The current initializer uses the named finite vertex field and active pressure support described in the authoritative addendum.

After the traction and cut-pressure spaces are corrected, pressure uniqueness should be established by an explicit nullspace/mean treatment if one is actually needed. A fixed physical value at an arbitrary interior point is not an appropriate general gauge for this transient free-surface problem.

### FS-16 — Current tests and qualification give false confidence

[contact_angle_free_surface_integration_20260615.md](qualification_logs/contact_angle_free_surface_integration_20260615.md) verifies that generated contact-line quadrature exists, that diagnostics report a 90-degree angle, and that an isolated residual is registered. It explicitly excludes dynamic wetting physics.

The evidence does not exercise the failures above:

- the angle is 90 degrees and the initial surface is already orthogonal, so the residual is approximately zero;
- γ=0, so no capillary equilibrium or Young force is tested;
- contact-angle unit helpers assemble the nonproduction **level_set** operator;
- diagnostics sum raw generated weights before StandardAssembler's incorrect remapping;
- no test changes θ and observes a change in the production **equations** residual or solution;
- no affine-mapping or mesh-scaling test exposes codimension/frame errors;
- no advancing/receding side-wall line, non-90 sessile drop, hysteresis, or slip benchmark is run;
- the so-called Laplace-law test cancels two boundary loads at zero internal pressure instead of testing a pressure jump;
- ambiguous saddle and aligned-edge intersections are absent.

Consequently, the qualification establishes geometry activation only. It does not qualify prescribed-angle enforcement or wetting.

## Baseline assessment of the D18/D38 side-wall error

The current evidence supports the following sequence:

1. Coupled level-set transport advances φ with LevelSetAdvectionVelocity in **equations**.
2. The advection velocity in dry cells is produced by nearest-interface copying. It has no wall compatibility condition and can be non-smooth near the gate, lid, and side-wall corners.
3. Continuous-Galerkin transport with the current SUPG scale has no bound-preserving mechanism. A steep gate cliff can therefore flip a dry positive nodal value negative.
4. The prescribed angle does not resist the flip because its residual is in the unsolved **level_set** operator.
5. Strong no-slip velocity on every wall pins a true material contact line but does not prevent φ at a dry wall node from changing due to the artificial extension.
6. Projection repair can propagate a bad reconstructed fragment into distance values; existing D18 evidence shows earlier false-wetting onset when it is enabled.
7. Cadence-one global volume correction preserves total negative volume by shifting all φ values, turning local false wetting into a global depression of the real free surface.

The repository report gives direct numerical evidence: dry lid/side-wall corner values changed from about +0.035 to −0.011 in ten steps while local fluid velocity was near zero; six false-wet nodes appeared by t=0.235 and grew thereafter. D38 did not show the same corruption, plausibly because its deeper bed produces a milder gate-cliff configuration. That contrast does not exonerate the algorithm; it shows sensitivity to geometry and transport state.

Items that are **not** the immediate D18 trigger:

- the capillary traction sign, because γ=0;
- the contact-angle sign, because θ=90 degrees makes the sign invisible;
- global volume correction, which begins as an amplifier after the local sign error;
- the pressure gauge pin, which prior ablations found corrupts local pressure but has negligible profile effect.

The highest-value diagnostic run is therefore not another angle-penalty sweep. It is a short D18 run that records, for every dry boundary vertex before its first sign change:

- φ old/new and each assembled transport contribution;
- source and extended velocity, chosen nearest sample ID and distance;
- u_ext·n_wall and local CFL/metric CFL;
- local gradient, SUPG τ, residual, and update;
- reinitialization and volume-shift increments separately;
- whether the angle residual row exists in **equations**.

## What appeared correctly implemented in the baseline

The audit also found several defensible pieces:

- LevelSetNegative and LevelSetPositive active-domain volume selection and normal reversal are internally consistent.
- The experimental cut-volume shape derivative has the correct side sign: −δφ/|∇φ| for the negative domain and the opposite for the positive domain.
- Zero external pressure and zero surface tension correctly need no explicit traction term; the natural zero-traction condition is sufficient.
- The external-pressure contribution −p_ext n has the correct sign.
- Curvature is flipped when the active positive phase reverses the interface normal, preserving internal orientation consistency.
- Raw level-set curvature with nonzero surface tension is guarded unless a projected or supplied curvature path is selected.
- Input checks correctly reject currently unsupported unfitted mobility and slip combinations rather than silently pretending to implement them.
- The qualification log honestly states that dynamic wetting, wall energy, and slip are outside its scope. The problem is that its operational conclusion goes beyond what its tests exercise.
- The SmoothedIndicator active-domain route is marked diagnostic rather than presented as a sharp cut-volume method.

These pieces should be preserved while the operator, geometry, sign, and physical-model contracts are repaired.

The energy formulations of Reusken--Xu--Zhang and Zhao--Ren integrate wall friction on the actual wetted wall and couple its boundary to the same Young/contact-line force; Zhao--Ren's unconditional global discrete-stability result is specifically for its Stokes scheme, while its Navier--Stokes relation carries moving-mesh interpolation error. That partition is geometric: replacing a level-set representation `phi` by `c phi`, `c>0`, must not change it. Sussman's signed-distance framework likewise makes physical smoothing widths meaningful only after `phi` is a distance (or is normalized by its gradient). The reviewed wall law therefore no longer applies a fixed physical width directly to raw `phi`; it uses a homogeneous signed-distance proxy and a compact dry tail. This is closer to the cited contract, but it remains a regularized wall-only approximation to a sharp cut-wall integral and does not import either paper's energy result.

The supported sharp-P1 path now couples the repository's selected Ren--E sign/mobility convention to wetted-wall Navier dissipation, uses a true `d-2` contact rule, assigns the default constant-`gamma` unfitted capillary force and its conormal to one generated `SurfaceStress` virtual work, and places per-event and cumulative bounds on maintenance displacement. Wet extension is also an algebraic unknown with an exact fixed-map `u -> E -> phi` chain and exact physical trace on retained cut support. These are necessary pieces reflected in the references, not a proof that the complete implementation inherits their energy, accuracy, or mixed-stability results. The candidate-rejection gate is a defensive nonlinear/time-step policy rather than the invariant-domain/FCT spatial methods above. The corrected physical matrices make the distinction concrete: wave and wall-transport isolations pass, but static equilibrium retains failed refinement gates and the completed moving-contact matrix fails three of four quantitative constitutive comparisons.

## Recommended target formulation

For an unfitted sharp-interface method intended to model wetting, a coherent target is:

1. **Bulk liquid equations** on Ω_l with the existing cut-volume restriction.
2. **Free-surface momentum force** from a sign-consistent variational surface-energy form. The scoped constant-`gamma` unfitted form is now implemented as `SurfaceStress`; if explicit curvature is retained, use σ_l n = −(p_ext + γκ)n for the current convention.
3. **Impermeable wall** u·n_w = wall normal velocity.
4. **Tangential wall law** using Navier/GNBC slip, weakly imposed in a cut-compatible way if necessary.
5. **Contact-line law** balancing contact-line friction and uncompensated Young force, with equilibrium angle θ_e and optional advancing/receding hysteresis.
6. **Level-set transport** by a controlled extension of the physical interface velocity, with wall-compatible extension and a boundary condition consistent with the selected dynamic-angle law. The current algebraic P1 extension meets the physical trace exactly on retained support at its frozen map, but D38 shows that the map builder is not yet controlled or bounded.
7. **Interface-preserving redistance/mass control** that reports and bounds zero-set and contact-line displacement.
8. **One weak-form/operator ownership contract** so every coupled term appears in **equations** and its dependencies are represented in the nonlinear tangent or an explicitly converged outer iteration. Operator routing and fixed-map algebraic coupling are implemented; generated-geometry shape derivatives remain outside the tangent.

Items 2, 5, and 8, plus the fixed-map algebra/trace portion of item 6, now have scoped implementations. The corrected `n=16` wave qualifies the item 2/6/8 capillary-transport trace in that case; it does not exercise item 5 or prove bounded extension-map construction. The completed true-sidewall advancing/receding matrix does exercise item 5 and fails three of four direct quantitative speed gates despite consistent bottom/vertical rotation. Broader physical refinement and energy qualification remain open. For a simpler first production milestone, qualify a pinned line and static capillary equilibrium separately. Do not label a no-slip, zero-surface-tension dam-break with a passive geometric angle as a wetting model.
