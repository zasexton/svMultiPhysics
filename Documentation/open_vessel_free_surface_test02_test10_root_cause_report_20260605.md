The active cut integration context is refreshed from the current FE solution before solves (`Code/Source/solver/Application/Core/ApplicationDriver.cpp:5031-5114`). The equation-level cut-domain validator explicitly warns that moving level-set cut domains only have first-order Hadamard cut-volume shape tangents and no full differentiated cut quadrature (`Code/Source/solver/Application/Core/LevelSetCutConfiguration.cpp:801-839`). For prescribed level-set data, the Navier-Stokes shape-tangent factor is not assembled (`Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp:1900-1917`); otherwise the unfitted shape tangent path is experimental and disabled by default unless enabled (`Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp:1900-1930`).

Inactive pressure DOFs are constrained through `LevelSetActiveSideVertexDirichletConstraint` when using cut-volume active domains (`Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp:3121-3172`). That constraint builds active support from retained cut-volume rules and cut-adjacent facets (`Code/Source/solver/FE/Constraints/LevelSetActiveSideVertexDirichletConstraint.cpp:488-570`) and then constrains DOFs without active support to zero (`Code/Source/solver/FE/Constraints/LevelSetActiveSideVertexDirichletConstraint.cpp:621-635`).

The pressure ghost penalty is assembled on cut-adjacent facets, not all facets. For enabled pressure stabilization, the code penalizes the jump of `grad(p)` in absolute mode or `grad(dt_eff * dt(p))` in incremental mode, scaled by `h^3 / (mu + eps)`, with an optional second-normal-derivative term scaled by `h^5 / (mu + eps)` (`Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp:1757-1789`). The underlying form uses `jump(grad(expr))` and `dS(facet_set_marker)` (`Code/Source/solver/FE/Forms/CutCellForms.h:181-213`). If metadata scaling is enabled, facet metadata supplies the stabilization scale (`Code/Source/solver/FE/Assembly/StandardAssembler.cpp:506-540`), and the global cap is 1e3 unless XML applies a lower cap (`Code/Source/solver/FE/Assembly/CutIntegrationContext.h:314-343`).

The unfitted free-surface boundary with zero external pressure and zero surface tension installs no explicit interface residual. The code logs `dynamic_stress=natural_zero`; if no dynamic stress and no kinematic enforcement are requested, it warns that the unfitted free surface installs no explicit `dI` boundary residual and returns (`Code/Source/solver/Physics/Formulations/NavierStokes/IncompressibleNavierStokesVMSModule.cpp:2757-2816`). This is mathematically plausible for a natural zero-traction free surface, but it increases reliance on the active cut-volume pressure/continuity path and pressure anchoring.

## Ranked Root-Cause Assessment

### 1. Active cut-volume pressure consistency

Likelihood: high.

The shared pattern in both tests is a pressure failure on the unfitted generated-interface active-domain path. Test02 produces a terminal MPa pressure maximum on a fully wet active node, while Test10 accepts a 495 Pa jump in one Newton iteration on an accepted state whose minimum active wet fraction is about 0.0447. This does not look like a single tiny-cut interpolation artifact.

The relevant code couples moving/generated cut-volume integration, inactive pressure constraints, pressure/continuity/VMS terms, and a refreshed-frozen geometry path. The current diagnostics show pressure jumps can be accepted with small residual norms even when the sensor or active-node pressure changes are physically implausible.

Counter-evidence: earlier Test02 controls do show tiny-cut pressure extrema, and Test10 failed retry contexts can include 1e-8-class moving trial cuts. Those remain real stability hazards. They do not explain the accepted Test02 final fully wet maximum or the accepted Test10 0.0447-min-fraction jump by themselves.

### 2. Pressure-row support/rank balance across Galerkin continuity, VMS/PSPG, ghost penalty, and constraints

Likelihood: high.

The VMS-disabled control sharpens this from a generic "active pressure path" concern into a row-support concern. Test02 still accepts a residual-consistent pressure excursion when VMS/PSPG is removed, and the worst active/wet jump grows from 2.112 MPa to 4.854 MPa. Test10 cannot even factor the direct system with VMS/PSPG disabled: the diagnostic reports 5 zero pressure rows and 5 zero pressure columns before acceptance.

The pressure-row support audit maps those five Test10 zero rows to dry/inactive source-state points only under a direct local-row-to-point assumption. The pressure constraint-coverage audit then compares the same rows with the solve-time pressure constraint immediately before factorization: `inactive_dof_runs=44-76|121-153|194-223|264-545|590-622|663-818`, so local rows `180`, `183`, `250`, `649`, and `652` are all outside the inactive pressure DOF constraint set. The same constraint reports `inactive_vertex_runs=168-545|630-818`, so direct VTU point support labels are marked unverified. A runtime sampled replay resolves the actual mapping: those rows are vertex DOFs for vertices `82`, `81`, `83`, `587`, and `586`, all with negative `phi`, `active_dof_support=1`, and `inactive_constraint=0`. A VMS-disabled matrix-support replay samples the same DOFs before the direct solve and reports zero row sum, zero column sum, and zero diagonal for all five. A VMS-enabled counterpart reports nonzero but tiny pressure-pressure row sums, with block sums `phi:0|Velocity:0|Pressure:<nonzero>`. The aggregate support-rank guard shows the zero-velocity-coupled class is larger than the singular subset: local pressure rows `180|183|190|193|250|649|652|659|662` are unconstrained and have zero `Velocity` row-block support in both enabled and disabled Test10 controls. Sampling all nine maps them to active-supported unconstrained vertex pressure DOFs; two are positive-side vertices retained through active support. Clamping those nine rows reduces, but does not remove, the accepted Test10 pressure update, so they are a proved rank/conditioning hazard rather than the only pressure-jump cause.

The row-level contribution diagnostic then shows the accepted full-wet pressure-jump rows are not driven by nonzero pressure ghost-penalty residuals: Test02 row `10676` has Galerkin continuity `-3.99815e-04`, VMS/PSPG `+3.99815e-04`, pressure ghost penalty `0`, and total residual `-7.59551e-12`; Test10 row `3356` has Galerkin continuity `-8.48247e-06`, VMS/PSPG `+8.48247e-06`, pressure ghost penalty `0`, and total residual `-7.44495e-13`. A Test10 weak-front contribution follow-up samples global pressure dofs `3353`, `3354`, `3356`, and `3358` / local rows `77`, `78`, `80`, and `82`; all four have pressure ghost penalty `0`, Galerkin/VMS cancellation, and total pressure residuals from `3.6e-14` to `1.2e-12`. The operator matrix-support replay extends the same ruling-out to the tangent rows at the two accepted maxima: pressure ghost penalty has zero sampled row support, while VMS/PSPG has the nonzero pressure self-block (`4.400190018e-10` in Test02 and `3.459819888e-8` in Test10). The VMS/PSPG split replay then shows the direct pressure-gradient split owns that pressure self-block, while the nonpressure split owns the velocity-coupling support and has zero pressure self-block. A scale-zero replay confirms raw removal is not viable: Test02 worsens to `37.486 MPa` and Test10 loses rank with the known five pressure zero rows. A scale-10 replay confirms rank-preserving strengthening is only directional evidence: it reduces the original sampled maxima, but leaves a `409.051 kPa` tiny-cut Test02 update and an `802.898 Pa` cut-supported Test10 update. A pressure-increment residual-form replay preserves the sampled pressure-gradient self-block but worsens both targets, proving that old absolute-pressure residual forcing is not the sole driver. A wall-boundary pressure-gradient support replay adds real pressure self support at the original Test10 max row and lowers that row, but it shifts the accepted maximum to global `3526` at `867.810372 Pa`; expanded coverage shows that operator covers most sampled important Test02 rows while specifically missing row `10676`, and covers the shifted Test10 row while leaving it zero-velocity-coupled.

This rules out "turn off PSPG/VMS" as a fix, rules out a nonzero ghost-penalty residual or matrix row as the direct accepted-jump-row driver, rules out the nonpressure PSPG residual terms as the pressure-self source, rules out raw deletion of the direct PSPG pressure-gradient term, rules out a uniform direct PSPG pressure-gradient scale multiplier as a complete fix, rules out a pressure-increment direct PSPG pressure-gradient residual form as a complete fix, rules out a simple wall-normal PSPG boundary support term as a complete fix, rules out inactive pressure identity coverage as the explanation for the sampled Test10 zero rows, and rules out a factorization-only reporting artifact for those rows. It strongly implicates coupled pressure-update/null behavior plus active pressure-row support/rank consistency in the direct PSPG pressure-gradient self-stencil. The assembled pressure equations can be either residual-consistent but physically implausible with the current pressure-gradient stencil, or rank-deficient on active supported vertex pressure rows when that pressure-gradient support is removed. For the sampled Test10 zero-coupling rows, the enabled support is pressure-pressure stabilization, not Galerkin velocity-pressure coupling. The accepted worst Test10 row is different: pressure-local row `80` / global `3356` has nonzero `Velocity` row-block support (`3.04958e-04`) and weak `Pressure` self support (`3.45982e-08`). A lightweight aggregate replay shows the 242 positive-coupled pressure rows all have small velocity-block row sums in this state (`1.40062e-05` to `1.17173e-03`). Targeted weak-coupling clamps show causality: threshold `3.1e-4` clamps 64 rows including row `80` and lowers the accepted jump to `352.269145 Pa`; threshold `3.3e-4` clamps 85 rows including the shifted row `78` and lowers the accepted jump to `182.117016 Pa`. The next shifted row is `82`, with larger velocity support (`7.26633e-04`). Rows `77`, `78`, `80`, and `82` all report retained volume fraction `1..1`, so weak coupling is not a tiny retained-support artifact. The all-pressure-row Test10 correlation links many of the largest pressure updates to weak or zero `Velocity` row-block support, but still finds above-threshold positive-coupled rows with updates up to about `618 Pa`. The Test02 all-pressure-row correlation is more decisive against a narrow zero-row fix: Test02 has no zero-coupled unconstrained pressure rows, and its largest full-wet update is a bottom-boundary row with positive velocity coupling (`2.60329e-3`) but weak direct PSPG pressure-gradient self-block support (`4.40019e-10`). Refreshed pre-solve support-rank joins now report the same class before acceptance in both cases. The constrained/unconstrained support split rules out constrained wall-velocity columns as the hidden source: constrained `Velocity` row/column support is zero for every sampled pressure row in both all-pressure replays, and the weak-pressure-self top updates and weakest self-block support-rank samples have zero constrained-velocity support. A diagnostic-only pressure self-block clamp strengthens causality for Test10 but rejects the raw threshold as a default fix: `row_self <= 1e-7` plus the zero-coupling clamp lowers Test10's worst update to `62.132386 Pa`, while `row_self <= 1e-8` in Test02 clamps 298 rows and produces a worse `2.80292 MPa` tiny-cut update. Counter-evidence: the baseline VMS-enabled pressure rows are numerically converged, so this is not a simple missing-stabilization or unconverged-residual story. It is a consistency and support/rank question in the formulation that defines those rows.

### 3. Pressure Ghost-Penalty Formulation/Scaling

Likelihood: medium as a branch shaper, low as a standalone fix target.

The pressure ghost-penalty variants have large, branch-like effects. In Test02, pressure penalty 10 suppresses official sensor peaks while worsening global pressure spikes. In Test10, disabled/zero penalty moves the one-step replay to a lower-pressure branch, penalty 0.001 overshoots worse, and penalties 0.01-0.25 stay on the high-pressure branch.

The implementation penalizes absolute pressure gradients by default on cut-adjacent facets. That can be correct for a stable unfitted method, but the artifacts suggest the current scaling/support/anchor combination is not pressure-consistent for these moving open-vessel cases. Treat this as a formulation issue, not a request for another penalty sweep.

The contribution diagnostic narrows the ghost-penalty role. The term is exactly zero at the accepted full-wet jump rows, so it is not the direct residual source there. It remains active elsewhere: it cancels most of the Test02 active-continuity residual at sampled row `11913` and dominates the Test10 residual-worst sampled row `3369`. That supports "branch-shaping mechanism" rather than "accepted-jump-row forcing term."

The active/wet branch-shaping audit closes the immediate policy-control question. In Test02, both pressure-disabled and pressure-incremental controls still trigger the `100000 Pa` guard and worsen the active/wet maximum, shifting it from a full-wet `2.112210 MPa` update to tiny-cut maxima of `9.135003 MPa` and `3.250406 MPa`. In Test10, pressure-disabled improves the same full-wet point from `1,075.558213 Pa` to `471.200656 Pa` but still triggers the `100 Pa` guard, while incremental worsens it to `1,613.511863 Pa`. Therefore pressure ghost penalty remains branch-shaping evidence, but the tested pressure-policy changes are ruled out as production fixes.

The full-gradient pressure-disabled replay rules out the same policy change under the stronger wall-gradient branch. With pressure ghost penalty disabled, exact top-row provenance reports `top_rows_direct_pspg_without_ghost_penalty`: both cases have `12/12` direct/wall PSPG weak-self top rows and no sampled ghost-penalty rows. Test02 removes the previous `5` ghost-positive top rows but worsens the accepted full-wet jump at row `10676` from `366719.965806 Pa` to `1298098.542745 Pa`; Test10 improves only from `622.609410 Pa` to `553.843093 Pa` and still triggers its `100 Pa` guard. This makes pressure ghost penalty a secondary branch shaper, not the primary fix path.

Counter-evidence: disabling pressure stabilization is not a validation fix. The Test10 zero-pressure/disabled variants either underpredict the 1 s peak or get the history timing wrong, so the pressure penalty cannot simply be removed without replacing the missing stability/consistency behavior.

### 4. Natural free-surface residual and pressure-anchor/support interaction

Likelihood: medium.

Both cases use zero external pressure and zero surface tension, so the unfitted free-surface boundary installs no explicit interface residual. The pressure field is then controlled by active-volume pressure/continuity terms, inactive pressure constraints, and any configured node pressure gauge. Test10 is clearly sensitive to the pressure anchor: the pressure0 1 s run changes peak amplitude and timing behavior relative to the cap-3 baseline.

Test02's obstacle pressure stack strengthens this concern: water reaches the P3 height by the saved cutoff, but P3/P1 is far too small. That looks like an incorrect vertical pressure distribution or pressure support/anchor behavior, not just a scalar offset.

Counter-evidence: natural zero traction is the expected weak boundary condition for a free surface with zero exterior pressure and no surface tension. The code path is not automatically wrong just because it has no explicit `dI` residual; the issue is the observed interaction with the moving cut-volume pressure solve. The diagnostic pressure-reference probe further narrows this: adding an explicit generated-interface `alpha * (p - p_ext) * q` term with `alpha=1e-6` leaves the worst updates above threshold and contributes `0.0` at Test02 row `10676` and Test10 row `3356`. The sampled bad rows are therefore not missing that direct free-surface trace reference. The pressure-subblock null diagnostic also reports near-zero signed pressure row sums and near-zero constant-pressure action at both sampled maxima, so those rows are not being forced by constant pressure/gauge leakage.

### 5. Time-step acceptance and terminal microsteps

Likelihood: medium as a trigger and guard gap, lower as the root formulation cause.

Test02's strict/pruned MPa spike is introduced by the final forced microstep, and Test10 accepts a large final jump after rejected attempts. This shows the nonlinear/time-step controller can accept pressure excursions that are hidden by the residual norm.

The accepted-update residual-context audit is captured in `Documentation/qualification_logs/open_vessel_free_surface_remaining_20260526/test02_test10_pressure_update_residual_context_20260607.json`. Test02 accepts a full-wet `2112209.8407043177 Pa` update after one converged Newton iteration with nonlinear field residual `1.8964751204792418`, a ratio of `1113755.6290063849`; Test10 accepts a full-wet `1075.5582134176257 Pa` update after one converged Newton iteration with nonlinear field residual `0.0011455415013957783`, a ratio of `938908.1164734041`. This supports keeping the pressure-update guard as a diagnostic safety gate, but it does not turn timestep rejection, residual tolerance, or smaller steps into the physics fix.

Counter-evidence: timestep changes do not close either case. Test02 half-dt still overshoots badly, Test10 smaller min dt worsens the pressure peak, and early-time Test10 dt overlaps are already close before the problematic 0.9 s window.

### 6. Ruled-out or lower-priority explanations

Sampling/postprocessing: mostly ruled out. Test02 offset, nearest-node, and local wet-node sampling still gives order-of-magnitude errors, and the worst final pressure is on a fully wet active node. Test10 pressure comparisons sample the intended Sensor1 path, and topology audits do not show accepted-output tiny-fraction support as the cause of the 0.900625 s pressure jump.

Old Test02 mesh resolution: ruled out as primary. h=0.10 improves some front metrics but not enough and does not fix pressure distribution.

Old Test02 rejected-step cluster: ruled out as sole cause. The half-dt control removes the old cluster and still accepts a large pressure jump.

Test10 body-force duration/source model: ruled out as current primary blocker. The full source and angular-velocity history are present.

Pressure sign convention alone: ruled out by the Test10 sign-flip replay, which leaves the high pressure essentially unchanged.
